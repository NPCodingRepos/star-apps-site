Kids learning apps without ads: how to check before you download

Five checks on the App Store and Google Play that show whether a kids app has ads, collects data or pushes purchases, before your child opens it.

Updated 2026-10-22

Ads in children's apps are more than an annoyance. They interrupt play, they often advertise other games, and one stray tap can take a child to a store page. The good news is that both app stores show most of what you need to know on the app's page, if you know where to look.

Five checks before you download

  1. Look for 'Contains ads'. On Google Play, apps that show ads carry a 'Contains ads' label on the app's page. If it is there, the app has ads, whatever the description says. The App Store has no such label, so read the description and recent reviews.
  2. Read the privacy section. On the App Store it is called App Privacy, and the best label for a kids app is 'Data Not Collected'. On Google Play, open Data safety and look at what is collected and what is shared.
  3. Check for in-app purchases. Both stores say when an app offers them. That is not bad in itself, since it is how many ad-free apps are paid for, but purchases should sit behind a parent check.
  4. Look for a parent gate. Good kids apps put purchases, settings and links out of the app behind a question a young child cannot answer.
  5. Check for sign-ups. A learning game for a five-year-old rarely needs an email address or an account.

Apple's rules for apps in the App Store's Kids category (App Store Review Guideline 1.3) say they must keep links out of the app and purchases behind a parental gate, and should not include third-party analytics or third-party advertising, with limited exceptions. In the United States, COPPA sets rules for collecting personal information from children under 13.

Why 'free' kids apps usually have ads

An app has to pay for itself somehow. Free apps usually do it with ads, or by selling extras to the player, which in a kids app means to the child. Ad-free apps charge the grown-up instead: a one-time price or a subscription. With a paid app you can see the deal before your child plays, and the app has no reason to keep them watching ads.

Turn on the store's own protections

  • On iPhone and iPad, Family Sharing has Ask to Buy: your child's purchase and download requests come to your phone for approval.
  • On Android, Google Play can ask for your password or fingerprint for every purchase, and Family Link lets you approve your child's purchases and downloads.
  • Screen Time on iPhone and iPad, and Family Link on Android, can set daily limits for apps.

Questions

How can I tell if an app has ads before I download it?

On Google Play, look for the 'Contains ads' label on the app's page. On the App Store there is no such label, so read the description, the App Privacy section and recent reviews.

What does 'Data Not Collected' mean on the App Store?

The developer declares that the app collects no data. It is the developer's own declaration, and Apple asks developers to keep it accurate and up to date.

Are apps in the Kids category always ad-free?

Not always. Apple's rules say they should not include third-party advertising, but allow limited contextual ads from ad services that have human review of ad creatives. Check the app's page.

Can we try Clever Cove for free?

Yes. Your child's first game is free. After it, a grown-up can start the Family Pass behind a parent check: all 14 games for up to 4 children, with a 7-day free trial.
