Virtual staging rules: MLS disclosure and what you can change

Is virtual staging legal? Yes, with care. What MLS rules and California's AB 723 ask you to disclose, what you must never change, and how to label photos.

Updated 2026-10-22

Virtual staging puts furniture into photos of an empty room, so buyers can judge the scale and the use of each space. It is legal and widely used. The rules around it are all about one thing: buyers must not be misled about the property itself.

Where the line usually falls

Usually fine, with a labelUsually not allowed
Adding furniture, rugs, art and plants to an empty roomHiding cracks, stains, damage or anything a buyer should know about
Removing the seller's furniture and clutterRemoving or changing permanent fixtures, such as built-ins or appliances that stay
A replaced grey sky, or a twilight version of a daytime photoChanging the view, removing power lines, or adding a lawn, a pool or a patio that is not there

Routine photo editing, like brightness, colour correction and straightening, is generally not treated as altering the picture. Changing what is in it is.

What MLS rules can ask for

Every MLS writes its own rules, and they change. Two examples:

  • CRMLS (California): Rule 11.5.2, in force since 1 January 2026, requires the original, unaltered photo immediately before or after the altered one in the listing, and a label such as 'virtually staged' or 'digitally altered' in the photo description. Virtually clearing a room of furniture that does not stay with the sale counts as altered too.
  • Stellar MLS (Florida): allows virtual staging of furnished or unfurnished homes, but photos must give a true and accurate picture of the property's features. You may add items that are not permanent and change furnishings that will not stay; you may not remove utility lines, add landscape plantings, cover up material flaws, swap out or remove permanent fixtures, or change paint colours.

Your MLS may also want a checkbox, a line in the public remarks or a label on the photo. Read its current rules before your first staged listing.

California's law since 2026

California's AB 723 (Business and Professions Code section 10140.8) took effect on 1 January 2026. When a licensee advertises a property with a digitally altered image, the ad must say that the image was altered and give access to the unaltered original, for example through a link or a QR code, next to the image. It covers changes made with editing software or AI that add, remove or change things in the picture, not routine adjustments like lighting or colour correction. It applies to your advertising in general, not only the MLS: your website, social posts and flyers too.

This is general information, not legal advice. Other states, MLSs and brokerages have their own rules, and the NAR Code of Ethics (Article 12) asks REALTORS to present a true picture in their advertising. When in doubt, ask your broker or your MLS.

A safe routine

  1. Keep the original of every photo you edit.
  2. Label each altered photo, on the photo or in its description: 'Virtually staged' or 'Virtually edited'.
  3. Put the original next to it in the listing when your MLS asks, and link to it from other ads where the law asks.
  4. Stage with furniture that fits the room: realistic sizes, nothing blocking windows, doors or features.
  5. Never use editing to hide a defect.

Questions

Is virtual staging legal?

Yes. What can break the law or MLS rules is using edited photos in a way that misleads buyers, or not disclosing them where a rule requires it.

Do I have to disclose virtual staging?

Many MLSs require it, and in California the law has required a disclosure and access to the original for digitally altered images in ads since 1 January 2026. Labelling staged photos is good practice everywhere.

Does a replaced sky count as an alteration?

A change that adds, removes or replaces something in the picture is the kind of edit these rules are about, while brightness or colour correction is not. The safe reading is to treat a new sky as altered and label it.
