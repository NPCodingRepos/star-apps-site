How to make a photo move like a video: 3 ways on your phone

Three ways to turn a still photo into moving video on a phone: a camera move through its depth, animating only the water or sky, and AI photo to video.

Updated 2026-10-22

There are three quite different ways to make a still photo move, and each suits different photos. A camera move keeps the photo as it is and travels through it. Animating part of it makes only the water, the clouds or the hair move. AI photo to video invents motion that was never there. Here is how each one works and when to use it.

MethodWhat movesBest for
Camera move (2.5D or parallax)The viewpoint: the foreground slides past the backgroundLandscapes, travel, portraits with distance behind

1. Move the camera through the photo

The oldest trick is a slow pan and zoom across a still photo, often called the Ken Burns effect after the documentary maker who made it famous. A 3D camera move goes further: the phone estimates the depth of every pixel, splits the photo into layers and moves a virtual camera, so near things slide past far things as in real footage. It works on old photos too, because the depth comes from the image itself. The 3D photo effect guide has the details.

The most dramatic move is the vertigo shot, or dolly zoom: the camera moves in while the zoom pulls back, so the subject stays the same size and the background seems to stretch away. Alfred Hitchcock made it famous in Vertigo (1958).

2. Animate only part of the photo

In a cinemagraph most of the picture stays still and one part keeps moving in a loop. On a phone you draw the direction of motion with arrows on the water or the clouds, and protect anything that should stay still. Follow the real direction of the flow, and keep the loop short.

If the photo is a Live Photo from an iPhone, the Photos app can also play it as a Loop or a Bounce: open it, tap the Live button and choose an effect. That uses the few seconds of real motion the camera recorded.

3. AI photo to video

Generative video models can create a few seconds of motion from a single photo: hair in the wind, a slow turn, waves rolling in. They run on servers, not on the phone, so the photo is uploaded. The results can look real, but the model invents what it cannot see, so check faces and hands, and label the result as made with AI where the platform asks.

Which one to choose

  • To keep it real, use a camera move or a cinemagraph: everything you see was in the photo.
  • For a landscape with water or sky, combine them: a slow push in and moving water.
  • For a person, a gentle camera move, or AI photo to video if you want them to move.
  • Five to eight seconds is enough for Reels and TikTok. Export at 9:16.

Questions

Can I make a photo move without AI?

Yes. A pan and zoom (the Ken Burns effect) works in almost any video editor, and an iPhone Live Photo can play as a Loop or Bounce in the Photos app. A 3D camera move uses a small depth model on the phone, but it does not invent anything new.

What is a cinemagraph?

A photo in which one part moves in a loop and the rest stays still, like water flowing past a person who does not move.

Is my photo uploaded?

Not for the 3D camera, Animate, skies or depth effects in STAR, which run on your phone. Only the AI Studio tools, such as Photo to video, send the photo, after you press Make, and the app asks first.
